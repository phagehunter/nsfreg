The restricted party lists, one by one

Appendix A of the DCL names lists from seven federal agencies. Here's what each one is, who maintains it, what kinds of entities appear on it, and where to verify the current version. (NSF's appendix names 12 list categories; the State Department publishes its debarred parties as two separate lists, so this site tracks 13.)

One search to rule most of them: the federal government's Consolidated Screening List (CSL) combines several of these lists (Entity List, MEU, Denied Persons, State debarments, nonproliferation sanctions, and more) into one official search tool. Note the CSL does not include every Appendix A list — the 1260H and 1286 lists, the FCC Covered List, the UFLPA Entity List, and CBP's WRO list must be checked separately at the links below.

U.S. Department of War

Sec. 1260H List (Chinese Military Companies)

Section 1260H of the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) for FY2021 — Chinese Military Companies

The Department of War's annual list of "Chinese military companies" operating directly or indirectly in the United States. Companies are designated for ownership by or affiliation with the People's Liberation Army, China's defense ministries, or the military-civil fusion enterprise. Includes major technology, aerospace, shipping, and AI companies.

Legal basis
Sec. 1260H, William M. (Mac) Thornberry NDAA for FY2021 (Pub. L. 116-283), codified at 10 U.S.C. 113 note
Updates
Updated at least annually until December 31, 2030
This snapshot
Federal Register notice of June 10, 2026 (Vol. 91, No. 111) · 80 entries
Retrieved
2026-07-09
For researchers: Many companies on this list have commercial subsidiaries, U.S. research labs, or academic partnership programs. A collaboration, sponsored-research agreement, or gift relationship with any listed company or its employees would fall under the NSF prohibition.

U.S. Department of War

Sec. 1286 List (Academic Institutions)

Section 1286 of the NDAA for FY2019, as amended — List of Foreign Talent Programs and Institutions

The Department of War's list of foreign entities — mostly universities and research institutions in China, Russia, and other countries — identified as posing a risk to Department-funded fundamental research, including institutions connected to foreign military or malign foreign talent recruitment programs.

Legal basis
Sec. 1286, John S. McCain NDAA for FY2019 (Pub. L. 115-232), as amended by Sec. 1746, NDAA FY2020
Updates
Updated periodically by the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense (Research & Engineering)
This snapshot
FY24 Section 1286 List (public release, version 2) · 134 entries
Retrieved

For researchers: This is the list most directly relevant to academic collaborations: it names universities. Co-authorship arrangements, joint projects, visiting appointments, or advising relationships involving these institutions on an NSF-funded project would fall under the prohibition.

U.S. Department of Commerce, Bureau of Industry and Security

Entity List

Entity List (Supplement No. 4 to Part 744 of the Export Administration Regulations)

The largest and most frequently updated list here: foreign entities (companies, universities, research institutes, individuals) subject to export license requirements because of activities contrary to U.S. national security or foreign policy interests. Spans 80+ countries; includes many foreign universities.

Legal basis
15 C.F.R. Part 744, Supplement No. 4 (Export Administration Regulations)
Updates
Amended frequently — often several times per month — via Federal Register rules

For researchers: Because the Entity List changes so often, always re-check the live eCFR version or the Consolidated Screening List before relying on any snapshot — including this site's.

U.S. Department of Commerce, Bureau of Industry and Security

Military End-User (MEU) List

'Military End-User' (MEU) List (Supplement No. 7 to Part 744)

Foreign parties that BIS has determined are 'military end users' — entities that represent an unacceptable risk of use in, or diversion to, military end uses in China, Russia, Belarus, Burma, Cambodia, or Venezuela.

Legal basis
15 C.F.R. Part 744, Supplement No. 7 (Export Administration Regulations)
Updates
Amended periodically via Federal Register rules

U.S. Department of Commerce, Bureau of Industry and Security

Denied Persons List

Denied Persons List

Individuals and companies whose export privileges have been denied by BIS order, typically following export-control violations. Mostly individuals and small firms worldwide, each with a specific denial period.

Legal basis
15 C.F.R. Part 764 (denial orders under the Export Administration Regulations)
Updates
Updated as denial orders are issued, renewed, or expire

U.S. Department of the Treasury, Office of Foreign Assets Control

E.O. 14032 Annex

Annex to Executive Order 14032 (Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies)

The annex of Chinese companies named in President Biden's 2021 executive order addressing the threat from securities investments that finance certain companies of the People's Republic of China — the foundation of the Chinese Military-Industrial Complex sanctions program.

Legal basis
Executive Order 14032 of June 3, 2021 (86 FR 30145)
Updates
The Annex itself is fixed; subsequent designations appear on the NS-CMIC List
This snapshot
Annex as issued June 3, 2021 (static document) · 59 entries
Retrieved

For researchers: Companies from this annex generally also appear on the NS-CMIC List, which is the actively maintained version. Check both.

U.S. Department of the Treasury, Office of Foreign Assets Control

NS-CMIC List

Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List

OFAC's actively maintained list of Chinese Military-Industrial Complex companies subject to U.S. investment prohibitions. Overlaps substantially with the E.O. 14032 Annex but includes later additions.

Legal basis
Executive Order 13959, as amended by Executive Order 14032

Updated as OFAC adds or removes designations

U.S. Department of State, Directorate of Defense Trade Controls

Debarred Parties (Statutory)

AECA/ITAR Statutorily Debarred Parties

Persons convicted of violating or conspiring to violate the Arms Export Control Act, and consequently prohibited from participating in the export of defense articles and services. Mostly individuals.

Legal basis
Sec. 38(g)(4) of the Arms Export Control Act; 22 C.F.R. 127.7 (ITAR)
Updates
Updated as debarments are imposed, rescinded, or reinstatements granted

For researchers: Debarred persons may later be reinstated — the DDTC page notes rescissions. Verify current status before drawing conclusions about a named individual.

U.S. Federal Communications Commission

FCC Covered List

List of Equipment and Services Covered by Section 2 of the Secure Networks Act

Communications equipment and services deemed to pose an unacceptable risk to U.S. national security — naming producers such as Huawei, ZTE, Hytera, Hikvision, Dahua, Kaspersky, and Chinese state telecom carriers, including their subsidiaries and affiliates.

Legal basis
Secure and Trusted Communications Networks Act of 2019, Sec. 2; 47 C.F.R. 1.50002
Updates
Updated as the Public Safety and Homeland Security Bureau adds determinations

U.S. Department of Homeland Security

UFLPA Entity List

Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) Entity List

Entities in or connected to the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region identified for involvement in forced-labor practices — mining, manufacturing, labor-transfer programs, and procurement of affected goods.

Legal basis
Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (Pub. L. 117-78), Sec. 2(d)(2)(B)
Updates
Updated by the Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force, typically several times a year
